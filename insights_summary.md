# CreatorPulse: Insights Summary

Based on 77,596 YouTube India trending videos plus a separate comment dataset. Findings show association, not cause.

1. Science & Technology videos have the highest median engagement (8.7%, n = 2,411), while News & Politics has the lowest (1.5%, n = 2,375).  *(see: Track Progress)*

2. Titles of up to 30 characters are associated with 52% high performers (n = 4,278) versus 16% for titles of 71-100 characters (n = 40,292). Short titles beat long ones in 7 of the 8 biggest categories; Science & Technology is the exception.  *(see: Plan a Post)*

3. Videos with 1-5 tags are associated with 35% high performers (n = 3,698) versus 19% for 16-30 tags (n = 39,264). The pattern holds in 5 of 8 categories, but not in People & Blogs, Science & Technology or Sports.  *(see: Plan a Post)*

4. Titles with a number are associated with fewer high performers (19% vs 31% without; n = 37,665 and 39,931). The direction holds in all 8 categories but the size varies from about 1 to 13 points. Question marks show no consistent effect (31% vs 25% overall, but they hurt in Comedy and 3 other categories).  *(see: Plan a Post)*

5. The strongest time slot with at least 20 videos, across all niches, is Saturday around 08:00 IST (median engagement 7.1%, n = 425).  *(see: Best Time)*

6. In the comment data, 'lofi' has the warmest audience (86% positive, n = 394) and 'cnn' the most critical (55% negative, n = 497), using the dataset's own labels. VADER rates cnn comments far more positive (48% vs 15%), showing it misses criticism and sarcasm.  *(see: Audience)*

7. The fastest-rising tags (e.g. 'indiawaali maa', 'gashmeer mahajani', 'barrister babu') are TV serial and actor names, so trends in this data follow shows, not evergreen topics. This is the dataset period, not today.  *(see: Topics to Ride)*

8. In a 1,000-comment sample, the most common request was follow-up videos and series (20 keyword matches) and the most common complaint was costs and subscription fees (21). The sample was deliberately enriched with negative and request-like comments.  *(see: Lab 6 files)*

**Limits:** trending-only data (success bias), about two years old, one sampled comment dataset, VADER is a baseline model.
